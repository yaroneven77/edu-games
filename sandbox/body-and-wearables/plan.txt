## Purpose and confirmed requirements

Create a new English vocabulary/spelling game for Israeli children, with predefined dictionaries for external body parts and wearable items.

- Panel 1: a text input for entering an English word and an Assist button.
- Incorrect spelling receives gentle feedback and an invitation to correct or replace the word.
- For a likely misspelling, say it needs correction and offer a hint without revealing or automatically applying the correct spelling. No credit is given until a valid form is typed.
- Panel 2: each correctly entered word is displayed.
- The character should gradually reveal or become dressed as correct words are entered, rather than showing the complete picture from the start.
- Confirmed implementation direction: add separate body/clothing pieces to build and dress the character. Do not implement this as merely revealing areas of a flattened picture.
- The second panel starts empty. Correct entries assemble the character piece by piece; no initial silhouette or fully visible base character.
- Accept entries in any order, including eyes before head or a hat before hair. Draw only named parts/items at their intended coordinates; do not require prerequisites or automatically credit/show neighbouring parts.
- Free-building mode is confirmed: any predefined word supported by the selected character can add its corresponding part or item. There is no prescribed target outfit.
- When two items occupy the same clothing slot, the newly entered item replaces the displayed one. Both words remain in the learned-word collection.
- Sessions are free play: show the learned-concept count and let the child choose when to start a new character. Do not automatically finish after a word quota or after completing level suggestions.
- Starting a new character resets the drawing, learned-word collection and count. Do not carry learned words across characters or persist them across visits.
- Animals means upright cartoon animals that can wear clothes, not realistic four-legged animals or humans in animal costumes.
- Animal characters use human-like cartoon anatomy, including hands, palms, fingers and feet, so all 27 mandatory body concepts work for them too.
- Create original layered SVG characters and clothing in code. Review samples before producing the complete 50-character library; no external image-generation service or illustrator is required for the chosen approach.
- Assist provides an English clue about a body part or wearable item.
- Assist also offers optional Hebrew translation and first-letter help, with stronger support suggested for beginners. These aids do not automatically submit or credit an answer.
- The requested picture library has five categories and ten pictures per category: Superhero, Cartoon, Anime, Animals, Manga; 50 pictures total.
- Anime uses colourful animation-style characters. Manga uses black-and-white comic-style characters. Both remain distinct from the five site UI themes.
- Vocabulary must come from predefined lists, not arbitrary runtime-generated answers.
- Include all four proposed wearable groups: clothing, footwear, accessories/headwear, and costume extras such as capes, masks and crowns.
- All 27 body-part concepts explicitly listed by the user are mandatory, including forehead, jaw, thighs, palms and heels. Do not reduce the game to the existing 17-word body bank.
- Version 1 uses exactly those 27 body concepts. Extra body terms and animal-specific vocabulary are deferred until after demo review.
- Provide selectable beginner, intermediate and advanced levels. These are English proficiency levels, not fixed age assumptions.
- Combine open vocabulary with progressive learning: accept any correctly spelled, supported dictionary word at every level; introduce vocabulary through level-specific suggestions, hints and learning targets. Advanced coverage includes all 27 required body concepts.
- A beginner who correctly types an advanced word still receives credit and its visual part. Do not reject a correct word merely because it is outside the current level's suggested subset.
- Accept singular and plural forms as the same learned concept, including irregular pairs such as foot/feet. Entering another accepted form must not award duplicate credit or add duplicate body parts.
- Ignore letter case and accept predefined common variants such as T-shirt/tshirt and trainers/sneakers, in addition to the agreed singular/plural forms. Genuine misspellings are not accepted.

## Predefined vocabulary - required body coverage and proposed additions

Version 1 includes exactly the required 27 body concepts plus the four wearable groups below. Extra body and animal-specific words are deferred until after demo review. All supported dictionary words are accepted at every level; allocate suggested learning targets and hints progressively without restricting correct free-form entries. Every character, including upright animals with human-like anatomy, must support all 27 required terms.

### Required external body parts - all 27 concepts

| Group | Required vocabulary, using the user's requested forms |
| --- | --- |
| Head and face | head, hair, forehead, eyes, ears, nose, cheeks, mouth, lips, chin, jaw |
| Neck and torso | neck, shoulders, chest, waist |
| Arms and hands | arms, elbows, wrists, hands, palms, fingers |
| Legs and feet | legs, thighs, knees, ankles, feet, heels |

- Give each concept its own vocabulary entry, Hebrew meaning, hint and identifiable visual region or detail.
- Do not merge chin with jaw, hands with palms, legs with thighs, or feet with heels. Related regions may share an anatomical parent but remain separate learning targets.
- All character artwork must support the complete required anatomy. Upright animal characters deliberately use human-like cartoon hands, palms, fingers and feet; do not reject a mandatory body word because an animal was selected.
- Add a coverage assertion for this exact 27-concept set; a total-count check alone is insufficient.
- Broad parts provide a neutral base shape; detailed vocabulary adds its own recognizable feature or region. For example, head does not pre-render eyes, ears, hair or lips. Hand does not automatically earn palms or fingers. Keep detailed layers above their parent shapes regardless of entry order.

### Deferred vocabulary - not part of version 1

Do not add face, tooth/teeth, toe/toes, eyebrow, eyelash, tongue, thumb, fingernail, back, belly or hip in the first version. Consider them only after the demo is reviewed.

Confirmed singular/plural policy; list these forms explicitly in the dictionary:

- eye / eyes, ear / ears, cheek / cheeks, lip / lips, shoulder / shoulders.
- arm / arms, elbow / elbows, wrist / wrists, hand / hands, palm / palms, finger / fingers.
- leg / legs, thigh / thighs, knee / knees, ankle / ankles, foot / feet, heel / heels.
- `hair` stays `hair` when referring to the hair on someone's head.
- Exclude internal organs such as heart and lungs from the visible-body dictionary.

Also defer the animal-specific entries:

paw, tail, wing, beak, fur, feather, hoof, horn, whisker.

Version 1 animals deliberately have the agreed human-like anatomy and use the same 27 body concepts.

Accepted common variants to encode explicitly:

- trainers / sneakers.

Canonical entries include a Hebrew meaning and explicit accepted forms. Do not treat all near-synonyms as universally interchangeable. In particular, do not automatically remove a trailing `s`: `glass` is not an alias for wearable `glasses`, and `short` is not an alias for `shorts`.

## Acceptance and hint design

Confirmed level behaviour:

- All levels accept the same complete supported dictionary.
- Beginner, intermediate and advanced introduce suggested vocabulary gradually, with level-appropriate hint difficulty and support.
- Every Assist clue offers optional Hebrew translation and first-letter help. Use authored clues, not random adjective/object combinations; all difficulty levels retain access to the optional aids.
- Suggested body progression: beginner focuses on head, hair, eyes, ears, nose, mouth, arms, hands, fingers, legs, knees and feet; intermediate adds cheeks, lips, chin, neck, shoulders, chest, waist, elbows and wrists; advanced adds forehead, jaw, palms, thighs, ankles and heels. These are cumulative hint pools, not restrictions on correct input.
- Wearable suggestions progress from familiar clothes/shoes/hats to more specific accessories and costume vocabulary. All 46 remain accepted at every level.

Input and hint rules:

- All 27 body entries have drawable layers on every character. Provide compatible wearable layers/adapters for the complete first-version inventory rather than exposing vocabulary with missing assets.
- A correct item does not need to be visible before submission: adding it is the intended action.
- Trim surrounding whitespace, normalize repeated spaces and ignore letter case. Accept only explicitly listed word forms; do not broadly strip punctuation or auto-correct spelling.
- Check exact accepted forms first. Confirmed: a likely typo prompts a retry and optional hint, without revealing its corrected spelling or silently receiving credit.
- For an unrecognized entry, use neutral wording such as "This word is not in our list. Check the spelling or try another word." Do not claim a correctly spelled but out-of-scope English word is a spelling mistake.
- Never expose an item with missing artwork as a normal learner error; validate vocabulary-to-layer coverage before shipping.
- Singular/plural aliases count as one concept (confirmed); do not introduce separate number-practice scoring.
- Assist selects an unfound suggested item and gives a semantically accurate clue; it is not limited to items already drawn. After the current level's suggestions are exhausted, offer additional unfound vocabulary without ending free play.
- A clue such as "You wear it on your head" is ambiguous if both a hat and a helmet are eligible. Add distinctive details or accept all valid answers.
- Because this is free building, another valid word remains acceptable while a hint is displayed. Clear the active hint when its concept is found; a hint is not a mandatory challenge or prerequisite.
- Example body clue: "This body part is between your eyes and your mouth. You smell with it." Answer: nose. Example wearable clue: "You fasten this around your waist to hold up trousers." Answer: belt.
- A duplicate or singular/plural alias does not increase the count. Re-entering a previously found but replaced garment puts it back on the character without counting it again.
- Confirmed: a learned-concept count with a manual new-character action, not timed or quota-based rounds. New character resets the drawing/list/count; do not add cross-character history, points, penalties, streaks or persistent progress.

## Proposed implementation structure

New preview path: `sandbox/body-and-wearables/index.html`. It has its own level selector and is linked only from the sandbox until approved for production. Final canonical placement can be decided during promotion.

- Standalone HTML game using existing shared themes and local SVG assets; no network requirement during play.
- Keep game content and logic self-contained in the HTML, following repository conventions. Use local SVG assets where useful; do not add a framework, build requirement or online service.
- Predefined vocabulary records such as `{id, category, canonical, acceptedForms, he, suggestedLevel, hints}`. Hint records include English text, Hebrew translation and accepted concept IDs where a clue can reasonably describe more than one item.
- Character records such as `{id, category, previewImage, altHe, altEn, supportedItemIds, layers}`. Each layer references its vocabulary concept, asset, normalized attachment point, clothing slot and draw order. Encode compatible layers and conflicts explicitly; same-slot clothing replacement never removes a concept from learned words.
- Round state includes `{characterId, level, learnedIds, activeSlots, activeHintId, feedback}`. Derive the count from the unique learned-ID set. New character clears all round-specific state.
- Separate RTL Hebrew instructions/feedback from LTR English input, clues and word labels.
- Support mobile input, Enter-to-submit, clear focus, optional pronunciation and non-colour-only feedback.
- Scope typed-input behaviour explicitly: this game intentionally requires keyboard/touch-keyboard input, unlike existing choice-only games.
