2. WORLD STORY AND VISUAL PURPOSE
============================================================

Gale Garden is a large floating botanical island suspended above a luminous cloud sea. Long ago, gardeners and weather engineers used brass wind beacons to guide gentle air currents through the island. Those currents watered the plants, carried seeds between terraces, powered the observatory, and kept the giant Sky Tree awake.

A recent storm twisted the paths, darkened the beacons, scattered seed pods, and covered the garden in moving cloud shadow. The island is damaged but not destroyed. It must feel recoverable, inviting, magical, and safe for children aged approximately 9-12.

The visual story has two main states:

STORM STATE
- Wind beacons are dark or weak.
- The Sky Tree core is dormant.
- Blue-grey storm clouds occupy parts of the upper sky.
- Grass and hanging leaves lean in the wind.
- Waterfalls remain active.
- Warm daylight still breaks through some clouds.
- The scene is mysterious and urgent, never frightening.

RESTORED STATE
- Six wind beacons glow mint-teal and warm gold.
- The Sky Tree core shines pale gold.
- Healthy green light travels through branch channels.
- Storm clouds thin and reveal a warm sun.
- Leaves, pollen, and small seed motes travel on smooth wind paths.
- A luminous green route stretches toward the next island.
- The same scene becomes brighter and more hopeful without changing object positions.

CARTOON-REALISTIC DEFINITION
Use believable materials, construction, lighting, depth, and weathering. Simplify and slightly exaggerate silhouettes so objects remain easy to identify when displayed between 64 and 300 pixels. Use soft painterly rendering, selective crisp edges, gentle texture, and controlled highlights.

Do not use thick black outlines.
Use dark coloured edge accents only where required for readability.

CAMERA
- Three-quarter elevated world view.
- Approximately 25-30 degrees above the horizon.
- Objects show their front, side, and a useful amount of top surface.
- Maintain the same camera angle for every freestanding world object.
- Background landscapes use matching perspective and horizon height.
- Avoid fisheye, extreme wide-angle distortion, dramatic Dutch angles, or top-down map views.

LIGHTING
- Primary daylight from upper left.
- Warm late-afternoon sunlight filtered through clouds.
- Cool sky fill from above.
- Mint-teal glow from restored wind technology.
- Pale gold glow from the Sky Tree and completed mechanisms.
- Soft contact shadows fall down and slightly right.
- Brass catches restrained warm highlights.
- Wet rock and waterfall edges catch small cool highlights.
- No harsh black shadows.
- No excessive bloom that erases silhouettes.

MATERIAL LANGUAGE

- Observatory: warm sandstone plaster, cedar beams, dark teal slate or metal roof, brass fittings, circular glass instruments.
- Garden structures: worn cedar, mossy stone, brass pins, pale rope, glazed ceramic irrigation pieces.
- Land: layered soil, brown and charcoal floating cliffs, roots, moss, grass shelves, occasional small crystal mineral seams.
- Sky Tree: believable bark, broad sculpted branches, wind-shaped leaf clusters, brass botanical channels integrated into the trunk, luminous core that feels ancient but engineered.
- Paper: warm parchment with weathered edges and abstract marks only; never readable writing.

SHAPE LANGUAGE
- Friendly technology uses circles, rounded hexagons, arcs, and softened compass shapes.
- Wind mechanisms use spirals, vanes, feather-like fins, curved channels, and directional chevrons.
- Buildings use sturdy triangles, circles, and gently tapered walls.
- Botanical forms use flowing S-curves, layered leaf fans, and wind-bent silhouettes.
- Ancient Atlas details use concentric circles, compass points, six-part radial patterns, and subtle geometric inlays.
- Broken or storm-affected states use diagonals and asymmetry but no dangerous spikes.

DETAIL HIERARCHY
- Large silhouette first.
- One clear focal feature second.
- Medium material details third.
- Fine texture last.
- Do not cover every surface with markings.
